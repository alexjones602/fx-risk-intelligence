# AI-Powered FX Risk Intelligence Platform

## Week 4 - Session 1: Forecast Accuracy and Model Performance

## 1. Executive Summary

The objective of Week 4 Session 1 was to evaluate and compare the forecasting accuracy of three models developed during Week 3 of the FX Risk Intelligence Platform:

- GARCH (1,1)
- Random Forest
- XGBoost

The models were evaluated using Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) to determine how accurately each approach predicted five-day FX volatility.

An additional forecast bias assessment was conducted to investigate whether the models systematically overestimated or underestimated actual volatility.

The evaluation covered 181 observations, with all three models assessed against the same actual volatility values.

The results identified GARCH as the strongest-performing model, achieving the lowest MAE and RMSE. XGBoost ranked second, while Random Forest produced the highest forecasting errors.

The forecast bias analysis also identified positive bias across all three models, indicating a tendency to overestimate actual volatility.

Overall, GARCH demonstrated the strongest initial forecasting performance and provides the benchmark against which future model evaluations will be compared.

## 2. Data Preparation and Quality Assessment

The evaluation used the existing `predictions.csv` dataset containing actual volatility observations and predictions generated by the three forecasting models.

The following variables were used:

| Variable | Description |
|---|---|
| Actual_Volatility | Actual five-day volatility |
| GARCH_Predicted | GARCH forecast |
| Random_Forest_Predicted | Random Forest forecast |
| XGBoost_Predicted | XGBoost forecast |

A common evaluation dataset was created by removing observations containing missing values across any of the four required columns.

This ensured that all models were evaluated against identical observations.

The resulting dataset contained 181 observations, with no observations excluded from the evaluation.

This provided a consistent basis for comparing the forecasting accuracy of all three models.

## 3. Forecast Accuracy Results

Two statistical measures were used to evaluate forecasting accuracy.

**Mean Absolute Error (MAE)** measures the average absolute difference between actual and predicted volatility. A lower MAE indicates greater average forecasting accuracy.

**Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE)** measures the square root of the average squared forecasting errors. It places greater emphasis on larger forecasting errors.

The following results were obtained:

| Model | Observations | MAE | RMSE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| GARCH | 181 | 0.001430 | 0.001708 |
| XGBoost | 181 | 0.001620 | 0.001929 |
| Random Forest | 181 | 0.001801 | 0.002072 |

### 3.1 GARCH Performance

GARCH achieved the strongest forecasting performance across both evaluation metrics.

Its MAE of 0.001430 was the lowest of the three models, indicating that its forecasts were closest to actual volatility on average.

Its RMSE of 0.001708 was also the lowest, suggesting that it performed better when larger forecasting errors were given greater weight.

Compared with Random Forest, GARCH reduced MAE by approximately 20.6% and RMSE by approximately 17.6%.

This suggests that GARCH was particularly effective at capturing the volatility patterns present within the evaluation dataset.

### 3.2 XGBoost Performance

XGBoost achieved the second-best results, with an MAE of 0.001620 and RMSE of 0.001929.

Although it did not outperform GARCH, it demonstrated better forecasting accuracy than Random Forest across both evaluation measures.

Its performance suggests that gradient boosting provided a useful machine learning approach to modelling FX volatility.

However, the results indicate that the current XGBoost implementation was not able to outperform the traditional econometric GARCH model.

### 3.3 Random Forest Performance

Random Forest produced the highest forecasting errors, with an MAE of 0.001801 and RMSE of 0.002072.

This indicates that its predictions were less accurate on average than those produced by GARCH and XGBoost.

The results suggest that the current Random Forest configuration may not be capturing the underlying volatility patterns as effectively as the alternative approaches.

Further investigation into feature selection, model parameters and forecasting performance across different periods may help explain its weaker results.

## 4. Model Ranking and Comparative Assessment

The models were ranked independently using MAE and RMSE, with the lowest error receiving first place.

| Model | MAE Rank | RMSE Rank | Overall Ranking |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| GARCH | 1 | 1 | 1st |
| XGBoost | 2 | 2 | 2nd |

Both evaluation metrics produced identical rankings.

This consistency strengthens the initial conclusion that GARCH was the most accurate forecasting model within the evaluation sample.

An important observation is that the machine learning models did not outperform the traditional econometric approach.

This demonstrates that greater model complexity does not necessarily translate into improved forecasting accuracy, particularly when modelling financial time-series data.

## 5. Forecast Bias Assessment

Forecast bias was calculated to investigate whether the models systematically overestimated or underestimated actual volatility.

The calculation used was:

**Bias = Mean(Predicted Volatility - Actual Volatility)**

A positive result indicates overestimation, while a negative result indicates underestimation.

The corrected Python implementation produced the following results:

| Model | Forecast Bias |
|---|---:|
| GARCH | 0.000810 |
| XGBoost | 0.000830 |
| Random Forest | 0.001019 |

### 5.1 Interpretation of Results

All three models produced positive forecast bias, indicating that they tended to overestimate actual volatility across the evaluation sample.

GARCH recorded the lowest forecast bias at 0.000810, suggesting that its predictions were closest to actual volatility in terms of average directional error.

XGBoost recorded a slightly higher bias of 0.000830, while Random Forest produced the highest bias at 0.001019.

Random Forest therefore demonstrated the greatest tendency to overestimate actual volatility.

### 5.2 Implications for Financial Risk Forecasting

Forecast bias is particularly important when applying volatility models to financial risk management.

Consistently overestimating volatility could result in more conservative assessments of market risk, potentially leading to higher capital allocation or more restrictive trading limits.

Conversely, underestimating volatility could result in insufficient risk protection during periods of market instability.

Although overestimation may provide a degree of conservatism, excessive bias can reduce the efficiency of risk management decisions.

GARCH demonstrated the lowest average bias alongside the lowest MAE and RMSE, providing further support for its initial performance advantage.

## 6. Key Findings

The analysis produced several important findings.

**1. GARCH was the strongest-performing forecasting model.**

GARCH achieved the lowest MAE and RMSE, indicating that it produced the most accurate forecasts across the 181 evaluation observations.

**2. XGBoost outperformed Random Forest.**

XGBoost achieved lower forecasting errors across both measures, suggesting that its boosting approach was more effective than the current Random Forest configuration.

**3. Both accuracy measures produced consistent rankings.**

MAE and RMSE independently identified GARCH as the strongest model, followed by XGBoost and Random Forest.

**4. All three models demonstrated positive forecast bias.**

Each model tended to overestimate actual volatility. GARCH produced the lowest bias, while Random Forest produced the highest.

**5. Traditional econometric modelling remained competitive.**

Despite the development of machine learning approaches, GARCH outperformed both Random Forest and XGBoost in this initial evaluation.

This highlights the continued relevance of traditional econometric models when forecasting financial volatility.

## 7. Limitations of the Evaluation

Although the results provide a useful initial performance assessment, several limitations must be acknowledged.

- The evaluation currently focuses on the currency pair represented in the existing prediction dataset rather than providing a separate comparison across GBP/USD, EUR/USD and USD/JPY.
- The analysis measures overall forecasting accuracy but does not establish whether model performance remains consistent across different market conditions.
- The evaluation does not yet establish whether the models generalise effectively to genuinely unseen future observations.
- Forecast bias measures average directional error and does not capture the full distribution of forecasting errors.
- The analysis does not establish whether differences in model performance are statistically significant.

Consequently, the results should be treated as an initial performance baseline rather than definitive evidence that GARCH will consistently outperform the machine learning models.

## 8. Overall Conclusion

Week 4 Session 1 successfully established an initial quantitative baseline for evaluating the forecasting accuracy of GARCH, Random Forest and XGBoost.

GARCH emerged as the strongest-performing model, achieving an MAE of 0.001430 and RMSE of 0.001708 across 181 observations.

XGBoost ranked second, while Random Forest produced the highest forecasting errors.

The additional forecast bias assessment supported these findings, with GARCH demonstrating the lowest average directional forecasting error.

An important conclusion from this session is that the traditional GARCH approach currently provides more accurate five-day FX volatility forecasts than the two machine learning approaches.

However, the results only demonstrate performance across the current evaluation sample. They do not establish whether GARCH will maintain its advantage when forecasting genuinely unseen observations or during different market conditions.

Further testing is therefore required before selecting a final forecasting model.
